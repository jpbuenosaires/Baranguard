## DILG MC 2024-086 (Omnibus Policy on Barangay Tanods, issued about 26 June 2024)
- URLs read: scribd.com/document/756176709 (truncated, metadata only); respicio.ph commentary; search summaries. **Secondary only.**
- Stated (secondary): the policy covers qualifications, recruitment, training, ethics, supervision, gender sensitivity, emergency/disaster response, and honoraria. Women are encouraged to serve, and recruitment may not discriminate on gender or sexuality. A tanod must be a Filipino citizen and resident, appointed by the Punong Barangay on BPOC recommendation. Benefit types listed: honorarium, duty allowance, equipment, accident insurance, meals during deployment, and awards.
- Honorarium: no minimum amount was found. A commentary says total honoraria must not exceed the SG-1 minimum (unverified). The P600/month floor is NOT confirmed.
- Number, age, training hours, accreditation and firearms provisions: NOT verified.
- LGU reporting requirements: not found.

## DILG MC 2003-42 (Professionalizing the Barangay Tanod, 10 March 2003)
- URLs read: scribd.com/document/341349426 (truncated); summaries. **Secondary only.**
- Stated (secondary): the Punong Barangay supervises and designates a Chief Tanod. Teams have a leader plus 2-4 members. Appointments run 3 years. Qualifications include residency, literacy and moral character. Duties are patrol, crime monitoring, assisting authorities and public safety. Benefits are honoraria, insurance, study grants and awards. Equipment is listed (nightstick, handcuffs, whistle, flashlight, first-aid kit, and so on).
- Numbers, prohibitions and reporting: not verified.

## DILG MC 2026-037 (Safer School Zones, 25 June 2026)
- URLs read: calabarzon.dilg.gov.ph press release (regional DILG, quoting the circular); search summaries. **Secondary, but quoting the circular directly.**
- Scope: 277,744 tanods across 42,010 barangays are mobilized as "force multipliers". Duties are visible presence, roving patrols, traffic assistance, immediate help, and prompt reporting/referral of incidents to the proper authorities.
- Limits (per the press release): operations are outside school premises, **except when authorized by school authorities or when immediate lawful assistance is necessary**. Tanods stay unarmed civilian support actors. Incidents needing police, fire, medical or social-welfare response must be referred immediately.
- Later news (Aug 2026 headlines on tanod outposts and deployment in schools, plus a Handbook reference to MC 2025-072 for tanods in public schools) suggests the outside-premises rule is evolving. **Not read; unverified.**
- LGU reporting: not found.

## DILG MC 2025-104 (LGUSS-BIMS) and its subsystems
- URLs read: bims.dilg.gov.ph; ncr.dilg.gov.ph Las Piñas orientation page; PIA (403) and Scribd (truncated). **Secondary (official DILG pages, not the circular).**
- The circular is titled "Guidelines on the Implementation of LGUSS-BIMS", with rollout starting January 2025 and an emphasis on data privacy compliance. LGUSS has two parts: BIMS (barangay) and CMIMS (city/municipality).
- The 11-subsystem count is repeated in news. The NCR page lists only 7: RBI (residents), BORIS (ordinances), BCCIS (clearances/certificates), **KPIS** (Katarungang Pambarangay case database), BAMS (assets), **BDRIS** (Barangay Disaster Resilience Information System), and GADPBMS (GAD plan/budget). The bims.dilg.gov.ph page adds technical support, custom website and report management. **I could not enumerate the full 11.** No dedicated e-blotter or tanod/dispatch module was found on those pages; the only incident-adjacent modules found are KPIS (disputes) and BDRIS (disaster planning/response data).
- Reporting: BIMS includes report generation for national agencies. Specific LGU obligations: not verified.

## NPC Circular 2024-02 (CCTV, effective 27 Aug 2024)
- URLs read: privacy.gov.ph PDF (403); divinalaw.com summary. **Secondary only.**
- Stated (secondary): the purpose must be legitimate, defined and disclosed, with clear signage. Capture only what is necessary, with no audio. Retain only as long as necessary for the purpose. Law-enforcement and court requests for footage are assessed case by case. Data subjects have access rights. The summary found nothing specific on minors, body-worn cameras, or barangays. The circular text itself may say more, so open the PDF.

## Implications for Baranguard
- **Requires or aligns:** unarmed tanods who report and refer incidents. This fits the system's dispatch, SOS and offline capture. Disaster/emergency reporting is a likely BDRIS overlap point.
- **Must not duplicate (BIMSS):** KPIS case records and any blotter/records list (already barred), resident profiling (RBI), clearances, and BDRIS-style disaster planning data. Position Baranguard as complementary (real-time dispatch, GPS, SOS, offline capture).
- **Safe to build:** patrol and shift scheduling, fatigue tracking, dispatch/SOS, school-zone patrol zone flags (outside premises by default), tanod training/accreditation tracking for local use (only after the real requirements are confirmed), and evidence/photo handling with minimal capture, retention limits and role restriction (aligned with NPC data minimization and the existing evidence rules). Any minors-in-photos rule needs the actual NPC and child-protection texts.
