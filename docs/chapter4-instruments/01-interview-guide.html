Key Informant Interview Guide

Form code BG-INT-01 | Baranguard: An Offline-First Barangay Intelligence and Emergency Dispatch System
Pilot barangays: Dao, Binanuahan, Marifosque, Banuyo (Pilar, Sorsogon) | Bicol University, IT 124 | Section 4.2 Data Gathering

Introduction

Baranguard is a locally hosted, offline-first barangay intelligence and emergency dispatch system for the four pilot barangays of Pilar, Sorsogon (Dao, Binanuahan, Marifosque and Banuyo). It supports real-time tanod dispatch, GPS tracking, SOS alerts and offline field capture. It complements the DILG Barangay Information Management System (BIMSS) and does not replace it: the barangay case records stay in the barangay's own records and in BIMSS. This form is one of the data-gathering instruments of the capstone study at Bicol University (IT 124).

Purpose of this form. To document how incidents, dispatch, rostering, record keeping, reporting and approvals are handled today, so that problems can be coded and mapped to the four specific objectives of the study.
Scope and respondents. Key informants in the four pilot barangays: the Chief Tanod, the Barangay Secretary, the Punong Barangay and a small group of tanods. Residents are not respondents.

Part 1. Instructions for the Interviewer

1.1 Purpose

This guide is used to document the current (as-is) process of incident and emergency response, tanod dispatch, rostering and availability, hours and accomplishment reporting, record keeping, referrals to other agencies, school-zone reporting, and approvals and signatures in the four pilot barangays. The answers are used to identify problems, which are coded and then mapped to the four specific objectives of the study. The interview does not test, promote, or evaluate any system. Respondents are the Chief Tanod, the Barangay Secretary, the Punong Barangay, and tanods. Residents are not respondents.

1.3 Before the interview

  • Secure the barangay official's permission and schedule a time and place free from interruption. Avoid the respondent's duty hours where possible.
  • Print one record header (Part 2), one consent block (Part 3), and the relevant role guide for each respondent. Bring a pen, a spare consent sheet, and a charged recorder (if recording is permitted).
  • Assign a respondent code (for example, DAO-A-01, BIN-D-02) before the interview. The code key is kept apart from the notes.
  • Use a neutral voice. Ask each question as written, then use the probes only if the respondent has not covered the point.

1.4 Consent script (read aloud before the respondent signs)

"Good day. My name is ______ , a BSIT student of Bicol University. We are conducting a study for our capstone project about how incidents and emergencies are handled in the barangay, such as how reports are received, how tanods are dispatched and scheduled, and how records and approvals are done. We would like to ask about how things are done now.

Your participation is voluntary. You may skip any question, and you may stop the interview or withdraw at any time without any consequence. There are no right or wrong answers. We are not evaluating you or your barangay.

Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act 10173), the information you give will be used only for this study and kept confidential. Your name will not appear in our manuscript. We will use only your role, for example 'Barangay Secretary,' and a respondent code. Please do not tell us about specific residents or cases by name; if you need to give an example, describe it without names.

With your permission, we would like to audio record this interview so that we can write down your answers accurately. The recording is used only by the proponents and the adviser, is stored on a password-protected device, and is deleted after transcription. If you do not want to be recorded, we will take handwritten notes instead.

Do you have any questions? Do you agree to take part? Please tick and sign the consent form."

1.5 Recording permission

Ask separately from the consent to participate. Tick the result in the record header. If permission is refused, take handwritten notes and do not record. If the respondent asks to pause the recording, pause it and note the pause in the margin.

1.6 How to anonymize

  • Record only the respondent code and the role label. Do not write the respondent's name in the notes, the quote boxes, or the tally sheet.
  • Replace names of residents, tanods, officials, streets, and sitios in quotes with bracketed labels, for example [a resident], [Tanod 2], [the barangay].
  • The manuscript uses role labels and respondent codes only, for example "Barangay Secretary, Respondent B-02."
  • Store the code key (code to name) on a separate sheet from the notes. Store recordings and notes on a password-protected device and delete recordings after transcription.
Your rights and our safekeeping. Under RA 10173 you have the right to be informed, to access and ask for correction of the information you gave, to object, and to withdraw your consent at any time without consequence. The information is shared only with the proponents and the capstone adviser, and only in anonymized form (role labels and respondent codes). Questions or requests: [name, role and contact of the proponents / adviser, to be filled by the proponents].

1.7 Conduct of the interview

  1. Read the consent script and obtain the signed consent (Part 3).
  2. Complete the record header (Part 2).
  3. Ask the questions in order. Questions are open; do not suggest an answer or imply that a problem exists. If the respondent says a step does not exist or is not done, record that as a finding.
  4. Write short notes under each question and use the verbatim quote box for statements worth quoting.
  5. End with the "Anything else" question and thank the respondent.
Language note. Questions are in English. Interviews may be conducted in Filipino or Bikol. Tanod questions carry a Filipino line under each English question. Guide A. Chief Tanod (system role: Admin, acting as Chief Tanod; operations seat)

Respondent code: __________   Barangay: __________   Ask about the current practice. Probes are optional; use them only if the respondent has not covered the point.

A1. Please describe how an incident or emergency is usually reported to you or to your tanods, from the first contact until a tanod is on the scene.
Probes: Who is usually the first to know? How do reports arrive (walk-in at the barangay hall or outpost, phone call, text, radio, social media, a tanod on patrol, other)? How are walk-in complaints handled? Who receives reports outside office hours?
A2. How is it decided which tanod responds to a report?
Probes: Who makes the decision? What does the decision depend on? How is the tanod informed? Can more than one tanod respond?
A3. How do you reach your tanods during a shift, and how do you know where they are? What phones, devices, and mobile load or data are used for this, and who provides them?
Probes: Which phones, numbers, or groups are used? Personal or barangay-owned phones? Android or other? How reliable is the signal? What happens when a tanod does not answer?
A4. Please walk me through how night-time and off-hours incidents are handled.
Probes: Who is on duty? How are they reached? Who decides to call additional tanods, and how are they asked? Who is informed afterwards?
A5. What does a tanod do when he or she needs urgent help while on duty, and what happens next?
Probes: Who is contacted first? What happens if there is no mobile signal or no load? Has this situation happened before, and how was it handled?
A6. How are tanod duty schedules (rosters) made, and who approves them?
Probes: How do tanods let you know when they are available? Who is consulted? Does anyone other than you draft the schedule? How are changes or swaps handled? How are tanods informed of their schedule?
A7. How are tanod hours and accomplishments recorded and reported, and who approves them?
Probes: What document is submitted, and how often? What evidence is required for the honorarium? Who signs it (paper signature or other), and what happens to the paper afterwards?
A8. After an incident is handled, how is it recorded, and where are the records kept? How is it handled when the same incident is reported more than once?
Probes: Who writes the record? Are photos or other evidence taken, and where are they kept? Is a blotter or binder used? How are earlier records looked up? How are repeated reports of one incident recognized? Who closes a case as resolved?
A9. What reports or annexes do you prepare or contribute to for the Punong Barangay or other offices, and how often?
Probes: Monthly reports? Summaries of incident trends or hot spots for the Punong Barangay? Reports under the Safer School Zones circular (MC 2026-037), such as school incidents and tanod school visits, if the barangay does this? Who compiles them and from which source?
A10. When an incident is too big for the tanods or belongs to another office (for example the police, fire station, ambulance or health unit, or social welfare), how is it passed on, and how is that recorded?
Probes: Who decides to call them? Who is called, and by what means? Is anything written down about who was informed and when? Is there a computer in the barangay hall?
A11. What concerns, if any, do you have about the privacy of incident information, tanod locations, or contact numbers?
Probes: Who is allowed to see incident details? How is information shared today?
A12. Is there anything else about how incidents, dispatch, scheduling, or records are handled that you would like to add?

Guide B. Barangay Secretary (system role: Secretary, Records and Reports Officer; records custodian)

Respondent code: __________   Barangay: __________

B1. Please describe your part in the handling of incidents and emergencies reported in the barangay, including walk-in complaints, and how the details are written down and by whom.
Probes: At what point do you become involved? How do you learn about an incident? What details are noted, and how soon? What happens to notes taken by tanods in the field? Who is allowed to read the full account?
B2. When an incident is passed on to another agency or office (for example the police, fire station, ambulance or health unit, social welfare, or a women and children's desk), how is that recorded?
Probes: Who informs the other office, and how? Is there a logbook or list of referrals? Is the outcome ever reported back? Who keeps the copy?
B3. How are the blotter or binder and other incident records kept, organized, and looked up?
Probes: Where are they stored? Who has access? How are older records and attached photos or evidence found when needed? How long are they kept?
B4. How are cases handled in which the same incident is reported by more than one person or in more than one way?
Probes: How are such reports noticed? What is written in the record? Who decides that one report is a repeat of another?
B5. What monthly reports, annexes, or other submissions do you prepare, and how are they put together?
Probes: To whom are they submitted and how often? From which sources do the figures come? How long does preparation take? What tools are used (paper, spreadsheets, word processor)?
B6. If the barangay reports under the Safer School Zones circular (MC 2026-037), how is that reporting done?
Probes: Which forms or annexes are used? Is there a list of schools in the barangay, and who keeps it? Who provides the information on school incidents and tanod school visits? How often? If the barangay does not do this, please say so.
B7. What is your role in the reporting of tanod hours and accomplishments, and in the honorarium documents?
Probes: What documents do you receive or prepare? What evidence is attached? Who signs them and in what order? Are signed paper copies filed or scanned?
B8. How do approvals and signatures on reports and rosters currently work?
Probes: Paper or digital? Who signs? How is the Punong Barangay reached when a signature is needed? What happens if he or she is not available? Who else may sign or note a document?
B9. What is your involvement in the preparation of tanod duty rosters?
Probes: Who prepares them? How do you learn when tanods can serve? Who approves them? How are they posted or sent to the tanods? How are changes and swaps recorded?
B10. What computers, phones, printers, software, and internet access do you use for your work, and how reliable are they?
Probes: Where are files stored? Are there backups? Who else uses the same devices?
B11. What concerns, if any, do you have about the privacy and security of the records you keep?
Probes: Who may read incident details? How are requests for copies handled? How are records shared with other offices?
B12. Is there anything else about record keeping, reporting, or approvals that you would like to add?

Guide C. Punong Barangay (system role: Punong Barangay, oversight; approves rosters and reports only when granted approval authority)

Respondent code: __________   Barangay: __________   If time is short, ask C1, C2, C5, C6, and C7 first.

C1. What is your role when an incident or emergency occurs in the barangay?
Probes: At what point are you informed or involved? Do you take part in deciding who responds? Does this differ between daytime and night-time?
C2. How are you informed about incidents and about what the tanods are doing?
Probes: Who informs you? How soon? In what form (call, text, report, meeting)? Do you know where the tanods are during a shift?
C3. How are night-time and off-hours emergencies handled in your barangay?
Probes: Who is responsible? How are responders reached? What is expected of the barangay leadership? If a tanod needed urgent help at night, how would you learn of it?
C4. What information do you rely on to oversee peace and order and tanod activity, and how often do you review it?
Probes: Where does this information come from? In what form is it given to you (for example summaries, trends by area or time, counts of incidents handled)? Where are incident records kept, and how do you access them when needed?
C5. How are tanod duty rosters decided and approved?
Probes: Who proposes the roster? What is your role (approving, signing)? How often is it revised? How are swaps handled?
C6. How are tanod hours and accomplishments reported to you, and what do you need to see before the honorarium is approved?
Probes: Which documents? How often? What does the treasurer or the auditor require? Who prepares and who notes the report before it reaches you? Is the signed paper copy the official one?
C7. How do approvals and signatures currently work, and how available are you to sign when something is needed?
Probes: Paper or digital? How do documents reach you? What happens when you are away from the barangay hall? Who may sign in your absence?
C8. What monthly reports and annexes does the barangay submit, and what is your part in them?
Probes: To which offices? Is the barangay reporting under the Safer School Zones circular (MC 2026-037)? If not applicable, please say so.
C9. When a matter is beyond what the barangay can handle (for example a fire, a medical emergency, or a serious crime), who is contacted, and what do you need to know about it afterwards?
Probes: Who calls the police, fire station, or ambulance? Are you told? Is there a record of who was informed and when?
C10. What phones, computers, and internet access do you and your barangay officials use for official work?
Probes: How reliable is the signal? Who pays for load or data?
C11. What concerns, if any, do you have about the privacy of residents' information and tanod information?
Probes: What would you want protected? What has the barangay done about this so far?
C12. Is there anything else about emergency response, oversight, or approvals that you would like to add?

Guide D. Tanods (system role: Tanod, mobile app; small group of 3 to 5)

Group code: __________   Barangay: __________   Number present: ______   Individual codes: ______________________________

Group facilitation notes.
  • Invite 3 to 5 tanods from the same barangay. Do not include the Chief Tanod, a barangay official, or a supervisor in the same group, because tanods may hold back in front of them.
  • State that the discussion is about how the work is done, not about anyone's performance. Nothing said will be reported to officials with a name attached.
  • Give each participant a code (D-1, D-2, and so on). Ask them to say the code before speaking if the interview is recorded.
  • Ask each question to the whole group, then invite quieter members by turn. Avoid letting one person answer for everyone. Note when answers differ.
  • Speak in the language the group prefers (English, Filipino, or Bikol). Use the Filipino line under each question as a guide; the Filipino text is a working translation, so rephrase naturally.
  • Remind everyone not to name residents and to keep the discussion within the group. State honestly that confidentiality within a group cannot be guaranteed.
  • Allow the group 40 to 60 minutes. Use a second interviewer or note-taker if possible.

D2. Once you know about it, how is it decided who goes to the scene?

Probes: Calls, text messages, group chats? What if your phone has no signal or battery?
D5. If you needed urgent help while on duty, what would you do, and what would happen next?
Kung kailangan ninyo ng agarang tulong habang naka-duty, ano ang gagawin ninyo, at ano ang susunod na mangyayari?
Probes: Whom would you contact first? What if there is no signal or no load? Has this happened to anyone you know?
D6. How do you learn your duty schedule, and how do you tell the barangay when you are available or not available?
Paano ninyo nalalaman ang inyong iskedyul ng duty, at paano ninyo sinasabi sa barangay kung kailan kayo available o hindi available?

D7. How are your hours and the work you did reported, and what do you need to submit for the honorarium?
Paano iniuulat ang inyong oras at ang mga ginawa ninyo, at ano ang kailangan ninyong isumite para sa honorarium?
Probes: What form or paper? How often? Who signs it? How long does it take to be approved?
D8. After you respond to an incident, what do you write down or report, and to whom? What do you do when there is no signal at that time?
Pagkatapos ninyong tumugon sa isang insidente, ano ang isinusulat o iniuulat ninyo, at kanino? Ano ang ginagawa ninyo kapag walang signal sa oras na iyon?
Probes: On paper, by text, by call? Do you take photos or voice recordings? How soon afterwards? Where do the notes go? What happens when the same incident is reported by more than one person, and how do you find out?
D9. What do you do when an incident is too big for the tanods, or happens at or near a school?
Ano ang ginagawa ninyo kapag masyadong malaki ang insidente para sa mga tanod, o nangyari ito sa loob o malapit sa paaralan?
Probes: Whom do you call (police, fire station, ambulance, barangay official)? Do you write down who was informed? Do you visit schools on patrol, and how is that reported?
D10. What phones do you use for duty, and how do you handle load, mobile data, and battery?
Anong cellphone ang ginagamit ninyo sa duty, at paano ninyo inaasikaso ang load, mobile data, at baterya?

Part 6. Report Format

Findings from the interviews are reported in the manuscript (Section 4.2) as follows. Counts, quotes, and problems come only from the completed forms. Nothing is written before the data exist.

  1. Respondent profile table: role label, barangay, number of respondents, years in role (range or mean), and whether the interview was recorded. Names are never shown.
  2. Theme table (Table 4-7): the completed Part 5 tally, with the representative statement and its role label.
  3. Narrative by topic: one paragraph per topic in Part 4, in third person and past tense, for example: "Of the [n] respondents, [n] reported that ..." followed by one short quotation labelled by role (for example, "Barangay Secretary"). Quotes are translated into English and the translator is acknowledged once in the methods text.
  4. Problem register: the problem codes P1 to Pn, each stated as an observed as-is condition with its source questions and the specific objective it relates to.
  5. Mapping table: problem code to specific objective (O1 to O4), then to the system component that addresses it, as confirmed by the proponents and the adviser.
  6. Limitations: number of barangays and respondents, the use of working Filipino translations, and the fact that the demo section, if used, was shown on fictitious data.
Facilitator notes.
  • Interview records are not shared with barangay officials in identifiable form.
  • Evaluators and respondents for the later ISO/IEC 25010:2023 evaluation must be external to the development team. Never invite or count group members as respondents.
  • The demo section is used only on the seeded demo setup (fictitious data), never on live records.
  • ISO/IEC 25010:2023 renamed Usability to Interaction Capability and Portability to Flexibility. Characteristic and sub-characteristic names must be checked against the purchased standard text before use.
