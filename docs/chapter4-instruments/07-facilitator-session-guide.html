Facilitator Guide: Running a UAT and Evaluation Session

Form BG-EV-02-G — for the proponents only. Do not give this guide to testers or evaluators.
Use it with the UAT form of the tester's role, BG-UAT-02-T, -CT, -S or -PB (tasks; the SUS is its Part D), and the questionnaire of the evaluator's group, BG-EV-02-A, -B, -C or -D (ratings). Scoring and the tally sheets are in section 10, the SMS run sheet in section 11 and the content validity rating sheet in section 12. Sampling is in the manuscript, Section 3.2.

Your job in one sentence. Set the stage, stay quiet, time the tasks, and write everything down. The tester marks Pass or Fail (standard UAT practice); you record the evidence. You do not help, hint, or touch the phone or keyboard. The tester must be a real user of that role. A member of the development team is never the tester.

2. Before the session

Room and people

A quiet place, with the tester sitting at the device and you sitting beside and slightly behind.

One facilitator. A second person may help with a stopwatch or the web side, but is not the tester.

Printed: the UAT form and the evaluation form of the tester's role, a pen, a stopwatch or phone timer, the defect log.

Data and system

Reset the demo data before each tester: double-click Reset UAT Data.bat (about one minute). Start within 9 hours of the reset, or the shift-based tasks fail for a data reason and not a defect.

Confirm you are on the demonstration setup with pretend data. No live barangay record is used. Tick this on the form cover.

Web dashboard opens and you are signed in on your own facilitator screen (Admin account) to watch and to trigger things.

Test account for the tester's role works. Write the password on a separate slip, never on a form.

Phone tasks: charged Android phone with the app installed, signed out, location on, mobile data or Wi-Fi on. Know how to toggle airplane mode and location quickly.

Write on the cover: system build, device and operating system, date, your name, tester code (for example UAT-01, no real name), start time.

3. Welcome and consent (say the same to everyone)

Say: “We are testing the system, not you. If something is hard, the system needs fixing. You can stop any time. Please do the steps yourself. I will only watch and write.”
“Ang sistema ang sinusubok namin, hindi kayo. Kung may mahirap, ang sistema ang aayusin. Maaari kayong huminto anumang oras. Kayo mismo ang gagawa; manonood at magsusulat lang ako.”
  1. Explain in plain words: pretend data only, no real personal data is typed, about how long it takes (from your pilot).
  2. Read the consent and privacy paragraph on the UAT form. Taking part is the tester's choice; they may stop or withdraw consent any time (Data Privacy Act, RA 10173).
  3. The tester ticks and signs. The name appears only on the signature page, never in the report. Everywhere else use the tester code.
  4. Give the account password on a separate slip. Take it back after the session.
  5. Ask the tester to say aloud what they are thinking. You write it in Remarks; you do not answer.

4. Running the tasks

For every task

  1. Read the task aloud (English, or the Filipino line beside it). Start the stopwatch when the tester begins.
  2. Watch. Do not point, hint or say “try the other tab”. If asked for help, say: “Do what you think is right.” and count it.
  3. Stop the stopwatch when the tester says they are done or when the result appears. Write the seconds.
  4. Ask the tester to compare the screen with What should happen (word for word where text is quoted) and to tick Pass or Fail themselves.
  5. Check the tick is on the right row. Do not change it. If what you saw differs from what the tester ticked, write that in Remarks; do not argue.
  6. For a Fail, write what happened in Remarks (the tester's words) and give a Defect ID (section 5).
  7. Add 1 to Errors or help every time the tester makes an error or asks you for help. A mistake that the tester notices and fixes alone still counts as an error.

Marking rules

SituationWhat you write
Screen matches “What should happen”The tester ticks Pass. Remind them once that Pass means the expected result is seen, not “close enough”.
Different result, step cannot be done, or an error showsThe tester ticks Fail; you write Remarks and the Defect ID.
Task not tried (time, device problem)Leave both boxes empty and write why.
Task depends on an earlier failed taskFail and write “blocked”.
Negative checks (T-N1, A-N2, C-N1, S-N1, S-N2, P-N1, P-N2)Pass means the system correctly refused or hid the thing. Read the expected result carefully before marking.
Failure caused by your setup, not the system (stale data, missing shift, wrong account)Fix the setup, tell the tester it was yours, and redo the task. Write “setup, redone” in Remarks. This is not a defect.

If something goes wrong

  • App or page freezes: wait one minute and note it as a possible defect. If it does not recover, restart once, write “restarted”, and continue.
  • Phone has no signal where it should: some tasks need a connection. Move to where there is one, or write the task as not tried.
  • Tester is stuck for more than a few minutes: ask them to mark Fail, write what they tried, and move on. Do not teach the answer.
  • Tester is upset or tired: offer a break or end the session. Their consent allows it. Never press them.
  • You notice real personal data on screen: stop, close it, and tell the lead proponent. Only pretend data should ever be present.

5. Defects

Every Fail goes in the defect log with a Defect ID that is also written in the task's Remarks. One defect per row.

SeverityMeaningExamples from the tasks
CriticalAn emergency or safety function is lost or wrong, data is shown to the wrong role, or the system cannot be used.SOS not sent (T-06 to T-08, C-02); a role sees a page it must not see (P-N1, S-N1); a dispatch cannot be assigned (A-03).
MajorA needed function fails or gives a wrong result, but there is a way around it.Offline report does not sync (T-18); wrong figures on the dashboard (P-02).
MinorA wording, layout or convenience problem that does not stop the task.A button label differs from the form; spacing.
Standard for acceptance: a pass rate of at least 90 percent and no critical defect still open. Pass rate = passed ÷ (passed + failed) × 100. Failed tasks are fixed and then run again; record the retest. Report both the first-run and the final result in Chapter 4. Count only the final result of each task after retests for the standard, and never delete the first-run mark.

Do not argue with the tester about a mark or about whether something is a defect. Write what they said and what you saw. The proponents decide severity after the session.

6. Evaluation form (ratings)

  1. Give the questionnaire of the same group: Tanod BG-EV-02-A, Chief Tanod and Secretary BG-EV-02-B, Punong Barangay BG-EV-02-C, IT practitioner or faculty BG-EV-02-D. Each form holds only that group's parts; end users already did the SUS and comments in UAT Part D.
  2. Read the “How to answer” box. One idea per statement; choose one box in each row; no right or wrong answers; N/A if not seen or not used.
  3. Give a respondent code (not a name) and write it on the form.
  4. Stay near but do not look over the tester's shoulder. You may explain a word, but never say what to choose.
  5. For Group D, show the evidence listed beside each statement. Tick Seen only for what was shown. Statements that cannot be shown in a session are marked developer-verified by you.
  6. Write “Reviewed by facilitator” and your name when you have checked that no row is left blank by mistake. Do not change any answer.

Scoring: section 10. Score the phone app and the web dashboard separately, and end users and Group D separately. Never pool them.

7. After the session

Thank the tester. Maraming salamat po. Tell them the results will be reported by code, not by name.

Collect the UAT form and the evaluation form. Check the cover, the tester code, the consent signature, and that the Pass/Fail column has no accidental blank.

Take back the password slip. Sign out of every test account on every device. Remove saved passwords.

Copy the Defect IDs into the defect log. Rate severity with the team the same day. Acceptance is signed by the business owner (the barangay official) in the sign-off part of the UAT form, not by the facilitator.

Enter the marks into the tally sheets and the SUS table (section 10) within a day, while you still remember context.

Store the paper forms in a locked place. The name appears only on the signature page, so keep that page apart from the answer pages.

Before the reset, run php backend/scripts/metrics-report.php --since= (the session date) and save the output with the session code. The reset wipes the session's GPS and sync rows.

Run Reset UAT Data.bat before the next tester.

Run failed tasks again after the fix and record the retest.

9. Quick card: do and do not

DoDo not
  • Say the same welcome every time.
  • Time every task and count every error or help request.
  • Write what you see, in the tester's own words.
  • Redo a task if the failure was your setup.
  • Keep passwords off the forms.
  • Touch the phone or keyboard during a task.
  • Hint, point, or explain where a button is.
  • Tick Pass or Fail for the tester.
  • Let a team member act as tester or evaluator.
  • Use or type real citizen, incident or personal data.
  • Change a tester's mark or answer, or write a name outside the signature page.
Open items that affect what you may report: the Filipino text is an unvalidated working translation until reviewed; the SMS fallback is not device-verified until the field test passes; the tanod and IT-expert sample sizes are open until the adviser confirms them.

Weighted mean and SD of each statement
