Introduction

Baranguard is a locally hosted, offline-first barangay intelligence and emergency dispatch system for the four pilot barangays of Pilar, Sorsogon (Dao, Binanuahan, Marifosque and Banuyo). It supports real-time tanod dispatch, GPS tracking, SOS alerts and offline field capture. It complements the DILG Barangay Information Management System (BIMSS) and does not replace it: the barangay case records stay in the barangay's own records and in BIMSS. This form is one of the data-gathering instruments of the capstone study at Bicol University (IT 124).

Purpose of this form. To confirm, role by role, that Baranguard does what the barangay needs. Each scenario is a short task with an expected result; the tester marks Pass or Fail and the facilitator logs any defect.
Scope and respondents. Testers are the people who would use the system: a Tanod, the Chief Tanod (Admin), the Secretary and the Punong Barangay. The test runs on the demo setup with fictitious data only.

Ground rules

  • The tester performs every step personally. The facilitator only observes and records and does not touch the device or keyboard.
  • Only fictitious data is used. No real personal data is entered at any time.
  • Every deviation, error message, confusion or workaround is written in the Remarks column and, if it is a defect, given a Defect ID that is also entered in the Defect Log.
  • Mark Pass only when the expected result is seen. Mark Fail otherwise. A step that is not attempted is left blank and explained in Remarks.
  • The tester must be the intended user of the role. A member of the development team may not act as tester.

Test accounts. Credentials are provided separately by the facilitator and are never written on this form.

The tester performs only the scenario table for the tester role (Part B, C and D, E or F). Other tables are left blank unless the facilitator assigns them.

How to Mark the Scenarios

  • Pass: the result on screen matches the Expected result column. Tick the Pass box.
  • Fail: the result differs, the step could not be done, or the screen shows an error. Tick the Fail box, write what happened under Remarks, and enter a Defect ID that is logged in Part H.
  • Leave both boxes empty only if the step was not run; the facilitator writes the reason. A step that depends on a failed step is marked Fail and noted as blocked.

Defect severity

SeverityMeaning
CriticalAn emergency or safety function is lost or wrong (for example SOS or dispatch does not work), data is exposed to the wrong role, or the system cannot be used.
MajorA required function fails or gives a wrong result, but there is a workaround.
MinorA wording, layout or convenience problem that does not stop the task.

Course standard (see Part G): all critical defects resolved and a pass rate of at least 90 percent.

Glossary of Terms

TermMeaning in this study
dispatchThe assignment of an on-duty tanod to an incident by the Chief Tanod (Admin).
dispatch offerA request sent to on-duty tanods at night (18:00 to 06:00, Manila time); the first tanod to accept receives the dispatch.
roster / published shiftA tanod duty schedule. A shift is a draft until an official with approval authority publishes it; a tanod sees published shifts only.
availabilityThe times a tanod states he or she can serve; the roster is built from it.
accomplishment reportThe monthly report of a tanod's work, prepared by the tanod, then noted and approved by officials. It serves as the tanod's attendance record.
referralA record that an incident was handed over to another agency (for example PNP, BFP or EMS). It records the handover, not the agency's acceptance.
Safer School Zones (Annex B, C-1, D)School-zone reporting: Annex B is the school list, C-1 the per-incident summary, and D the term report.
SOSAn emergency alert raised by a tanod from the phone. If the server cannot be reached, the phone sends an SMS to one configured backup contact.
demo setupA copy of the system loaded with fictitious data, used for testing and evaluation. No real record is used.
