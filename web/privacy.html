Privacy notice

This dashboard is used by barangay staff to dispatch Tanods, record incidents and keep barangay records. It holds personal information, so access is limited by role and records are kept for set periods.

What this dashboard holds

  • Incident reports: the type, place, time and status of an incident, and the names and contact numbers of complainants and respondents where recorded.
  • Confidential narratives: the written account of an incident. Only the Barangay Secretary can read it.
  • Tanod positions: the location of Tanods on duty, while they are on duty, so they can be dispatched and kept safe.
  • Duty hours: shift schedules, duty status changes and accomplishment reports of Tanods.
  • Citizen reports: reports sent in by residents, with the contact details they gave.
  • SMS records: messages sent and received through the barangay's SMS gateway, and the list of residents who asked to receive barangay alerts.
  • Staff accounts: names, usernames, roles, and official titles printed under signatures.

Why we hold it

To respond to emergencies, to dispatch and account for Tanods on duty, to keep the barangay's official incident records, and to send safety alerts to residents who asked for them. The Local Government Code of 1991 makes the Barangay Secretary the custodian of barangay records.

How long we keep it

  • Confidential narratives: deleted from the system 90 days after the incident is recorded, unless the record is under legal hold. The barangay keeps any official blotter in its own binders. [to be confirmed by the barangay]
  • Incident records: kept 7 years, as the barangay's official record.
  • Tanod positions, duty status, shift schedules and notifications: kept 1 year.
  • SMS message log and unconverted citizen reports: kept 1 year.
  • Audit log: kept 7 years.
  • Accomplishment reports, availability, school check-ins, referrals and Annex D term reports: [to be confirmed by the barangay] (no deletion is done yet).
  • Backups: [to be confirmed by the barangay]

Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), you may:

  • Ask to know what personal data the barangay holds about you, and how it is used.
  • Ask for access to a copy of your personal data.
  • Ask for correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
  • Object to the processing of your data in certain cases.
  • Withdraw consent, for example to receive SMS alerts. Ask to be removed from the list; your number is marked opted-out.
  • Complain to the National Privacy Commission if you believe your rights were violated.

Some records cannot be deleted on request while the law requires the barangay to keep them (for example, incident records under the 7-year retention, or a record under legal hold). The barangay will tell you if this applies.

This list of rights is to be checked against RA 10173 Section 16 before the notice is adopted.
